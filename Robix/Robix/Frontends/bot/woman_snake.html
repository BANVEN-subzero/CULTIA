The Woman and the Snake

A Fulani Tale of Wit and Wisdom

Back to Folklore

The Encounter on the Plains

In the vast, sun-drenched plains of Northern Cameroon, there lived a Fulani woman named Amina, known for her sharp mind and calm demeanor. One afternoon, while gathering firewood, she encountered a large, silver-scaled serpent coiled across her path. The snake, a mystical creature of the grasslands, spoke with a voice like the dry wind through grass.

The Challenge of Truth

The snake refused to move unless Amina could tell it three truths that it had never heard before. Amina didn't panic. She sat down and said, "First, a person's true wealth is not in their cattle, but in their character. Second, the shortest path between two people is a kind word. And third, even the most powerful serpent cannot strike if it is entangled in its own pride."

The Reward of Wisdom

The serpent was so impressed by Amina's quiet confidence and the depth of her words that it uncoiled and bowed its head. Before sliding away into the tall grass, it gifted her a small, smooth stone that glowed with a soft light. The stone, it said, would always guide her home when the dust storms obscured the horizon. Amina returned to her village, her wisdom celebrated by her people.

Cultural Significance

This Fulani tale highlights the value placed on intellectual agility and moral strength over physical force. In many Northern Cameroonian cultures, storytelling is a primary tool for teaching ethics and social values. The snake often appears as a dual-natured symbol: a dangerous predator but also a keeper of ancient, earthly secrets.
